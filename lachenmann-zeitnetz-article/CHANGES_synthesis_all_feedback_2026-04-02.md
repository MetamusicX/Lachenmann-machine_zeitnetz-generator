# Paper Revision — Synthesis of All Feedback

---

## Consensus Across All Three

### 1. Downgrade "vibe coding"
All three agree. Martin finds it unsubstantiated and potentially contentious; Adam questions the disclosure risk; Diego says make it a short methodological note. Don't build the paper's identity around it.

### 2. Reframe the paper's core
Diego is clearest: this is a paper on **computational reconstruction + musicological interpretation**. Adam says: artistic research, not technical report. Martin implicitly agrees by pushing for more musicological depth.

### 3. The strongest insight is the gap between grid and composition
Diego identifies this explicitly as the paper's best idea. Martin says the "generative disappearance" section is good. This should become the centre of gravity.

---

## Structural Changes

### 4. Modular architecture (Adam)
Separate the aesthetic/musicological argument from the technical pipeline documentation. Technical details can live in GitHub.

### 5. Add analytical demonstration (Diego)
Show concretely how the grid is deformed in the actual score. This is the missing piece: not just "the grid disappears" but *how* it disappears, with examples.

### 6. New section on heuristic search (Adam)
The discovery that not all inputs work is a genuine finding from reversing analysis into generation. Deserves its own treatment.

### 7. Clarify the gesture early (Adam + Martin)
Take the reader by the hand before diving into stages. Explain what this paper does and why.

---

## Conceptual Refinements

### 8. "Constraint as creativity" is not enough (Martin)
This is a common idea. What is *specifically Lachenmannian* about it? Does he position himself relative to others?

### 9. Distinguish from set theory (Adam)
Lachenmann borrows 0–11 numbering but his method is fundamentally different from Forte/Lewin/Babbitt. Make this explicit.

### 10. Correct Pena/Cavallotti attribution (Adam)
Cavallotti's work is foundational; integrate into the narrative.

### 11. Introduce "sound families" properly (Martin)
Don't assume the reader knows the concept.

### 12. Language consciousness (Martin)
The computational vocabulary ("input", "pipeline") applied to compositional process needs justification. Is this Lachenmann's language, Pena's, or yours? If yours, discuss it.

### 13. Signpost the verification result early (Martin)
The fact that the code matches Pena's final schemes is key. Tell the reader before the pipeline description, so they can compare as they read.
